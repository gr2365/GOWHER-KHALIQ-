1. What is Physical Geography?

Physical geography is the study of Earth's natural features, environments and processes. It asks how land, air, water, soil and living things work and change, and how they connect with one another.

2. Earth as a System

A system is a set of connected parts that exchange energy and matter. Earth's main spheres interact continuously:

  • Lithosphere: the solid outer Earth, including rocks, landforms and tectonic plates.
  • Atmosphere: the envelope of gases around Earth.
  • Hydrosphere: Earth's liquid water, including oceans, rivers, lakes and groundwater.
  • Cryosphere: frozen water, such as glaciers, ice sheets and sea ice.
  • Biosphere: living organisms and the places where life exists.

Example of interaction: Rain from the atmosphere flows over the lithosphere into rivers, while plants in the biosphere take up water and return some of it to the atmosphere through transpiration.

Earth's interior

From the surface inward, Earth has a thin crust, a thick mostly solid but slowly deforming mantle, a liquid iron-rich outer core, and a solid inner core. Heat from Earth's interior helps drive tectonic activity.

3. Rocks, Plates and Landforms

The rock cycle

Rocks are continually changed by Earth's internal heat, pressure, weathering, erosion, burial and melting.

Rock typeHow it formsExample
IgneousMagma or lava cools and solidifies.Granite, basalt
SedimentarySediments are deposited, compacted and cemented; some form from chemical or biological material.Sandstone, limestone
MetamorphicExisting rock is changed by heat, pressure or fluids without fully melting.Marble, slate

Plate tectonics

The lithosphere is divided into moving plates. Most earthquakes, volcanoes and major mountain belts occur near plate boundaries.

  • Divergent: plates move apart; new crust can form, commonly at mid-ocean ridges.
  • Convergent: plates move together; one plate may subduct beneath another, or continents may collide and build mountains.
  • Transform: plates slide past one another, producing earthquakes.

Weathering, erosion and deposition

Weathering breaks rock down in place. Erosion removes and transports material by water, wind, ice or gravity. Deposition occurs when transported material is dropped and accumulates.

4. Atmosphere, Weather and Climate

Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions. Climate describes typical patterns and variability over a long period, commonly assessed using about 30 years of records.

Most clouds, rain and day-to-day weather occur in the troposphere, the lowest atmospheric layer. Above it are the stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere and exosphere.

Elements of weather and climate

Temperature, air pressure, wind, humidity, cloud cover and precipitation are key elements. Solar energy is the main external energy source for weather and climate.

Why places have different climates

  • Latitude: affects the angle and concentration of incoming sunlight.
  • Altitude: temperature generally decreases with height through much of the troposphere.
  • Distance from the sea: water heats and cools more slowly than land, moderating nearby temperatures.
  • Ocean currents: redistribute heat and influence nearby coasts.
  • Relief and prevailing winds: mountains can lift moist air, causing rain on windward slopes and drier conditions in rain shadows.

5. The Water Cycle

Water continually moves between the atmosphere, land, oceans and living things. The Sun supplies energy for evaporation, while gravity moves water downhill.

  1. Evaporation: liquid water changes to water vapour; plants also release vapour by transpiration.
  2. Condensation: water vapour cools and forms tiny droplets or ice crystals in clouds.
  3. Precipitation: water falls as rain, snow, sleet or hail.
  4. Infiltration and percolation: water enters the soil and may move down to groundwater.
  5. Runoff and storage: water flows to streams and oceans or is stored in lakes, glaciers and aquifers.

Key terms: A drainage basin is the area drained by a river and its tributaries. A watershed is the boundary separating one drainage basin from another. An aquifer is permeable material that can store and transmit groundwater.

6. Soil and the Biosphere

Soil is a living, changing layer made from mineral particles, organic matter, water, air and organisms. It forms through interactions among climate, organisms, relief, parent material and time.

A simple soil profile may contain an organic-rich surface layer, topsoil, subsoil, weathered parent material and underlying bedrock. Not every soil has clearly developed versions of all these layers.

Climate, water, soils and relief influence where plants and animals can live. In turn, vegetation protects soil from erosion, adds organic matter and affects local water movement.

7. Quick Revision

  • Physical geography studies natural processes and environments.
  • Earth's spheres are connected; change in one sphere can affect others.
  • Igneous rocks form from cooled molten rock; sedimentary rocks from accumulated material; metamorphic rocks from changed existing rock.
  • Plate boundaries are divergent, convergent and transform.
  • Weather is short-term; climate is long-term patterns and variability.
  • The water cycle links evaporation, condensation, precipitation, infiltration and runoff.
  • Weathering breaks rock down in place; erosion transports material; deposition lays it down.
